# Sichuan Basin Induced Seismicity Catalog (2009-2018)

## Overview

High-precision earthquake catalogs from the Sichuan Basin, southwestern China, documenting the growing induced seismicity linked to shale gas development and industrial operations.

## Geographic & Temporal Information

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|

| **Center Coordinates** | 28.1°N, 104.9°E |

| **Time Period** | 2009-2018 (catalog); activity increased significantly post-2015 |

| **Magnitude Range** | ML ~0.1 to ~1.0 (catalog); up to Mw 4.7 (dense network) |

## Files in This Catalog

### 1. CEDC catalog.xlsx
- **Type**: Regional network catalog
- **Coverage**: 2009-2018
- **Events**: ~2,290
- **Columns**: Date, Time(UTC+8), Latitude(°), Longitude(°), Depth(km), Magnitude(ML)
- **Authority**: China Earthquake Data Center (CEDC)

### 2. Local network catalog.xlsx
- **Type**: Dense local seismic network
- **Coverage**: High-resolution observations
- **Format**: Temporary network deployments
- **Improved**: Better depth resolution, magnitude precision

### 3. Injection data of N5&N7.xlsx
- **Type**: Wastewater injection operational data
- **Wells**: N5 and N7 injection facilities
- **Data**: Injection rates, volumes, pressures, timing
- **Purpose**: Correlation analysis with seismic activity

## Seismic Context

### Induced Mechanism
- **Primary cause**: Shale gas hydraulic fracturing (hydraulic stimulation)
- **Secondary cause**: Long-term wastewater injection
- **Historical context**: 
  - Salt mining injection: >70 years (pre-industrial seismicity era)
  - Natural gas field injection: ~40 years
  - Shale gas development: Rapid growth since 2008 (especially post-2015)

### Key Temporal Evolution
- **Pre-2015**: Low, stable seismicity (background)
- **2015-2018**: Unprecedented continuous growth in earthquake frequency and magnitude
- **Spatial evolution**: Seismicity migrated and expanded following injection well locations

## Catalog Structure

```
CEDC catalog.xlsx (Regional Network)
├── Date: YYYY-MM-DD
├── Time(UTC+8): HH:MM:SS.s
├── Latitude(°): WGS84 latitude
├── Longitude(°): WGS84 longitude
├── Depth(km): Hypocentral depth
└── Magnitude(ML): Local magnitude

Local network catalog.xlsx (Dense Array)
├── High-resolution hypocenter locations
├── Improved depth accuracy (<1 km)
└── Better magnitude discrimination

Injection data of N5&N7.xlsx
├── Well ID: N5, N7
├── Injection timestamp: Date, time
├── Injection rate: bbl/day or equivalent
├── Injection pressure: MPa
└── Cumulative volume: Million barrels
```

## Suggested Metrics

### Highly Computable
- ✅ Magnitude-frequency analysis (b-value, Mc)
- ✅ Temporal decay (aftershock-like behavior post-injection pulses)
- ✅ Spatial clustering (convex hull, alpha-shape)
- ✅ Correlation with injection operations

### Good Candidates
- ✅ PCA elongation (linear alignment to injection well arrays)
- ✅ Migration patterns (radial expansion from wells)

### Challenges
- ⚠️ Mainshock-aftershock classification (swarm-like with multiple peaks)
- ⚠️ Depth determination (limited velocity model constraints)

## Regional Classification

**Sequence Type**: Induced Seismicity / Shale Gas Development

**Tectonic Setting**: Continental basin, Upper Yangtze Platform

**Stress Regime**: Regional compression, locally modified by pore pressure

## Applications

1. **Induced seismicity monitoring**: Real-time hazard assessment for energy operations
2. **Injection-seismicity coupling**: Quantify relationship between operational parameters and seismic response
3. **Hazard mitigation**: Evaluate traffic-light protocols (injection rate/pressure adjustments)
4. **Stress field analysis**: Understand pre-existing fault orientations reactivated by injection
5. **Cross-basin comparison**: Compare with Oklahoma/Kansas, Alberta, and other induced seismicity zones

## Important Notes

- Magnitude estimates may change as velocity models are refined
- Hypocentral depths are uncertain in early catalogs (pre-2015) due to limited network
- Injection data access may be restricted; available data is from published scientific studies
- Some events are triggered directly by hydraulic fracturing; others may be delayed pore pressure diffusion responses
